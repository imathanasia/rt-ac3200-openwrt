# Writing notes

The README and the release announcement were rewritten in a plain, first-person voice before
publishing. No tool was used for it; these are the rules that were applied, kept here so later
edits stay consistent.

- Write as the person who did the work. "I flashed it", not "the image can be flashed".
- Lead with what happened, including the failures. The two dead flashes and the radio-disabled
  discovery are part of the story and make the rest believable.
- Concrete numbers only, and say where they came from (unit, `dmesg`, `iw`).
- No marketing adjectives: no "seamless", "robust", "comprehensive", "cutting-edge".
- Avoid the "not just X, but Y" construction, and avoid three-item lists that exist only for rhythm.
- Short sentences mixed with longer ones. If every paragraph has the same shape, rewrite it.
- State limits before someone asks: 40 MHz not 80, no hardware NAT offload, 256 MB RAM.
- Never claim something works if it was only seen once, and say when it was not tested at all.

The Reddit post follows the same rules, with a shorter version for non-specialist subreddits.
